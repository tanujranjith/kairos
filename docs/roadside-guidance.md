# Rural roadside guidance

Kairos derives a continuous delineator layer from authored road progress on Lakeshore Drive, Lake Crossing, Ridgeway Pass, Pinecrest Road and Orchard Way. White posts use dark reflector bands and two outward-facing amber lenses, so their cadence gives open roads a stronger sense of scale by day and keeps road edges readable in rain, dusk and night driving. Ridgeway's two strongest curvature clusters additionally receive six yellow-on-black chevron boards whose outside placement and arrow direction derive from signed spline curvature.

Posts are forty-two metres apart and sit beyond the recoverable gravel shoulder. Generation excludes the first and last forty-eight metres of each route, water, bridge and tunnel spans, and protected junction sightlines. Chevron candidates require at least 0.0065rad/m authored curvature, remain at least twenty-eight metres apart and sit beyond the outside shoulder. Urban streets retain their separate streetscape system; highways, circuit roads and private access routes retain their barrier treatment. Every post and sign is deterministically assigned to the 256m cell containing its actual world position.

The guidance is visual, not an obstacle. White shells, supports and dark panels merge into the existing road-paint vertex-colour batch; amber lenses and double-sided chevrons merge into the existing centerline batch. It therefore adds no material, texture, light, draw call, external asset or Havok body. Road, shoulder, terrain, traffic and tire-contact geometry remain unchanged.
